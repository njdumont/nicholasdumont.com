Currently reading

  • The Penguin History of the World – wide-angle view of human history; I use it as scaffolding for everything else.
  • Probability and Statistics (DeGroot & Schervish) – core probability and inference; slow, dense, and worth it.

Recently finished

  • Macroeconomics (Mankiw) – standard macro toolkit; useful mainly as a baseline for how the "official" story is taught.
  • Modern Principles of Economics (Cowen & Tabarrok) – a different angle on micro/macro with clearer framing in some areas.

High-signal recommendations

These are books I’d recommend broadly. If I wouldn’t press it into someone’s hands, it doesn’t go here.

  • Debt: The First 5,000 Years (David Graeber) – forces you to see money, obligation, and states as historical, not just technical.
  • Fooled by Randomness (Nassim Taleb) – a decent vaccine against confusing luck with skill, especially in markets.
  • Principles (Ray Dalio) – not scripture, but a useful way to think about explicit decision rules and feedback.
