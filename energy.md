## Energy & markets

Energy is the base layer of the modern economy. The interesting part is not
“renewables vs fossil” in the abstract, but how specific market rules,
physical constraints, and behaviors interact in real time.

## Questions I keep coming back to

- In practice, how “real-time” are real-time markets, and what does that
  imply for forecasting, hedging, and true edge?
- How do different market designs (day-ahead vs real-time, nodal vs zonal,
  capacity constructs, scarcity pricing) change who takes risk and who
  captures value?
- Where do quantitative models actually improve decisions, and where do
  they just give unjustified confidence about noisy inputs?
- How will storage, flexible demand, and more granular pricing reshape the
  structure of power markets over the next decade?

## Why I work here

Getting energy wrong has first-order consequences for industry, logistics,
data centers, and households. Sitting close to the grid and its markets is
a good vantage point for understanding how the rest of the system behaves.
